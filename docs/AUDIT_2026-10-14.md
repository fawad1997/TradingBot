## Deliberately NOT done

**Breakeven `activate_r` 0.5 → 0.3.** Proposed, measured, withdrawn. Backtest on candidate-rule signals suggested a large gain; live data contradicted it. Median MFE of a losing trade is **0.04R**, so a 0.3R trigger would have caught **6 of 45** losses. The backtest's 36% became 13% in production because `micro_scalper` — 38 of the 45 — has a median losing MFE of 0.02R. Its entries are wrong from the open; no exit rule repairs that.

Two errors of mine surfaced on the way and are recorded because they affect how much the numbers are worth: the first simulation charged one spread and no slippage, overstating the benefit (train period went from +124.7R to −149.6R once slippage was added); the second treated every bar opening beyond the stop as a gap filled at the open, which fires constantly for a near-market stop and penalised only the breakeven variant.

**Removing the DEMO / DRY_RUN environment separation.** It is not scaffolding. `EXECUTION_ALLOWED[REAL]` admits only `MICRO_LIVE` and `VALIDATED`, and all eight strategies are `DEMO`, so REAL mode would execute nothing until strategies are promoted on recorded stats. Startup also refuses when the environment and the account type disagree, in both directions. That is the mechanism that makes a credential swap deliberate rather than accidental.

**Percentile thresholds for ADX and RSI.** 39 of 43 parameters are already multiples of a live quantity (ATR, a standard deviation, a rolling average) and cannot go stale. Only `h1_adx_min: 15`, `h1_adx_max: 18` and `rsi_extreme: 30` are fixed levels (`rsi_reclaim: 50` is the RSI midline, not a tuned value). A percentile threshold guarantees a fixed signal rate whatever the market does, losing the ability to go quiet — judged not worth it on two strategies with three trades between them.
